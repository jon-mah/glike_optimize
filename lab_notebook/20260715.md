    * [(REVIEW) `brandt2023promise.pdf`](../biblio/brandt2023promise.pdf)
      * Summary: Ancestral recombination graphs (ARGs) introduce a paradigm shift to human evolutionary genomics comparable to the coalescent. The primary improvement is inference of genealogical history rather than reducing genomic data to summary statistics.
      * Traditional methods infer genealogical history indirectly through summary statistics like the SFS, LD (linkage disequilibrium), haplotype length(s), F-statistics (like FST), or PCA (principal coordinate analyses).
      * Rather than utilizing nth-degree summary statistics, the ARG is nth-minus-one, i.e., one "level" closer to the actual biological process: Observed genome --> mutations --> underlying (true) ARG --> forces of demography, recombination and selection.
      * ARG advantages:
        * incorporation of recombination
        * increased statistical power; demographic events influence branch length, tree topology, recombination breakpoints / endpoints, local and global tree structure
        * ARG interpretability, e.g., analysis of Ne, migration, introgression, relatedness (and recency of relatedness), local / global ancestry, selection, mutation, age of mutation, genealogical neighbors / nearest neighbors
        * increased temporal resolution
      * ARG challenges
        * exact inference is computaitonally impossible / nonfeasible (hence annealing strategy)
        * difficult to quantify uncertainty
        * large sample sizes
        * large parameter space
        * recombination hotspots (irregularity of rate of recombination across genome)
        * sequencing errors
    * [`fan2022genealogical.pdf`](../biblio/fan2022genealogical.pdf)
        * introduces framework for measuring genetic relatedness using ARGs, rather than directly observing genetic variants
        * genetic variants are imperfect observations; however, by first estimating the ARG and then computing relatedness, we obtain a relationship matrix that is more robust to ascertainment bias, missing variants, and stochastic variation in mutations
        * eGRM (expected GRM) based on ARGs, averaging over all mutations
          * Infer ARG from genotype data
            * Relate or tsinfer+tsdate
            * Traverse _every_ branch in _every_ local tree
          * Assume mutations occur Poisson distributed across ARG tree branches
            * Weight each branch by length (generations), genomic interval, and mutation rate
          * Compute expected contribution _of each branch_ to pairwise relatedness
            * For each branch, identify descendants which inherit that branch
            * Add the branch's expected contribution to the relationship matrix
          * Integrate over all possible mutations on the inferred genealogy to obtain the expectation of the conventional GRM conditional on the inferred ARG.
        * Limitations
          * accuracy depends on the inferred ARG
          * Errors in ARG inference will propogate downstream
          * ARG inference is currently computationally demanding

        * As mentioned in the reviews, observed mutations only provide indirect evidence of a genealogy, whereas the genealogy itself is the undelrying process which generates genetic variation
        * For a genealogy with a known topology and branch length(s), the probability of observing the given sequence of coalescent events depends on the effective population size (Ne) through time, i.e., Ne(t)
          * Larger population --> longer waiting time between coalescent events and vice versa
        * constant-size piece (epoch)-based demographic inference (like in dadi or fastsimcoal)

    * [`tang2025genealogy.pdf`](../biblio/tang2025genealogy.pdf)
      * ARG-based framework for studying population structure of admixed populations.
      * ARGs used to characterize how different ancestral populations contribute to present-day genomes
      * Essentially extension of ARG-based demographic inference to include ancestry (multiple ancestral populations)
        * Traditional analyses consists of inferring local ancestry, and then analyzing ancestry tracts using allele frequencies or PCA
        * ... Why don't we just analyze the ARGs?
      * Genealogies (ARGs) provide a richer / more detailed representation of ancestry than local ancestry tracts, which fail to capture differences between similar ancestry groups that have distinct demographic histories
      * Method
        * Infer ARG from sampled genomes
        * From local genealogies across the genome, compute genetic relatedness for different ancestral backgrounds
          * How do lineages cluster within genealogical trees across genomic regions?
        * Summarize genealogical relationships to identify ancestry-specific patterns of population structure, i.e., estimation of ancestry-specific genetic relationships
          * for each ancestral background, we compute how closely related individuals are within the corresponding part of the local genealogy
          * because recombination yields different genealogies across the genome, the approach integrates information from many many local trees across the genome
          * distringuish between recent vs. shared ancestry, different subgroups within the "same" ancestry, and heterogenous demogrpahic histories within ancestral populations
      * Advantages
        * higher resolution inference of ancestry-specific structure than allele frequency methods
        * better characterization of admixed populations (evolutionary relationships amongst ancestry tracts
      * Limitations
        * dependent on accuracy of ARG
        * ARG inference is computationally expensive
        * sequence errors or errors in local genealogies can reduce power

## Review of literature review

My key takeaway is that literature is pointing towards a shift away from using observations of polymorphism data as input (SNP-based methods) and towards using genealogical relationships across the genome as input (ARGs). This is limited by the need for accurate ARGs (similar to how other methods rely on accurate SNP data), and the need for computaitonal complexity (due to the superscaling nature of ARG parameter space contingent on sample size and recombination events).

This clearly outlines the motivation of the project, being to optimize ARG inference out of / away from local minima while also improving upon computational inefficiencies.
